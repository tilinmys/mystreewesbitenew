## Shared direction — include in every generation

Create a photorealistic editorial photograph for MyStree, a welcoming women's health brand. Contemporary Indian women with varied skin tones, body types and ages; respectful, believable everyday styling. The visual feeling is calm confidence, dignity, autonomy and human connection. Soft diffused daylight, natural skin texture, believable anatomy and candid expressions. White architectural surfaces dominate, with oatmeal linen, very restrained dusty terracotta fabric and a small muted sage detail. Use the colors #FFFFFF, #EFE9E1, #8A5A44 and #2C6E49 as scene-styling references, not literal color overlays. Skin must retain natural tones. Premium documentary campaign photography, subtle depth, understated color grading, no heavy orange filter.

These are fictional illustrative campaign scenes. Do not imply the depicted people are actual MyStree clinicians or patients. Do not display patient records, credentials, clinic signage or readable screen content.

For desktop, position the people and the important action in the RIGHT 50–55% of the frame. Leave the LEFT 40–45% as naturally bright, quiet white wall or softly out-of-focus space for website copy that will be added separately. Leave the top 12–15% visually quiet for the floating navbar. Allow about 10% extra framing around important subjects for later crop, slow zoom and lateral drift. Do not crop heads, hands or eye contact near an edge. Keep important faces in the central vertical region so a wider hero crop remains usable.

Do not generate text, captions, typography, logos, buttons, navigation, decorative UI, watermarks, medical diagrams or artificial gradients. Avoid glamour retouching, plastic skin, beauty advertising poses, hospital emergency imagery, exaggerated smiles, pink-everywhere styling, malformed hands, duplicate limbs and invented anatomy. Generate still photographs with crisp subjects; Codex will animate the image layers later, so no baked motion blur is needed.
